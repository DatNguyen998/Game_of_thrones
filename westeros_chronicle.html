A Maester's Chronicle CITADEL RECORD · WESTEROS · YEARS 298–305 AC
Being a true account of the war for the throne

Nine Houses,
One Chair,
No Survivors Untouched.

Peace held Westeros together for seventeen years on a lie. When the lie surfaced, five kings reached for the same chair, a girl in the east raised three dragons, and something far older than any of them woke up north of the Wall.

It began, as these things do, with a death that wasn't an accident. Jon Arryn, Hand of the King, was poisoned for what he'd learned: that Queen Cersei's children were not King Robert's. When Robert rode north to make his old friend Eddard Stark the next Hand, he was asking a proud man to guard a secret that would burn the realm down anyway.

What follows is the short version — five turns of the wheel, a ledger of who was left standing, and an accounting of the seven whose choices decided most of it.

I–V

Five Turns of the Wheel

298–305 AC
I298 AC

The Hand's Duty

Ned Stark found his answer — Cersei and her brother Jaime, not Robert, had fathered every child on the throne's future — and did the honest, fatal thing: he told the wrong person before he had the power to act on it. Robert died on a hunt Cersei didn't discourage. Ned was arrested for treason and, against every promise made to spare him, beheaded on the steps of Baelor's Sept by his own successor, a thirteen-year-old sadist named Joffrey.

Ned Stark's execution turned a succession dispute into a war.

II298–300 AC

The War of the Five Kings

Robb Stark won every battle he fought and lost the war anyway, married for love instead of the alliance he'd promised the Freys, and paid for it at his uncle's wedding: Robb, his mother Catelyn, and his army's officers were slaughtered by their own hosts under a peace banner. Renly Baratheon was murdered by a shadow with his brother Stannis's face. Joffrey ruled Blackwater Bay's survivors with a cruelty that made his own family flinch.

The Red Wedding ended House Stark's army in one evening.

III300–303 AC

Winter Descends

Joffrey was poisoned at his own wedding; his uncle Tyrion took the blame, was sentenced to die, and killed his father Tywin on his way out of King's Landing for good. Across the Narrow Sea, Daenerys Targaryen freed three slave cities and learned that ruling is harder than conquering. At the Wall, Jon Snow made peace with the wildlings, broke the Night's Watch's oldest rule to do it, and was stabbed to death by his own brothers for the trouble.

Every storyline was now, quietly, about who could survive winter.

IV303–304 AC

Fire and Blood Converge

The Red Woman brought Jon back. He retook Winterfell from Ramsay Bolton with Sansa's army of Vale knights and was crowned King in the North for it. Cersei, cornered and out of allies, blew up the Great Sept of Baelor with wildfire — killing the High Sparrow, the Tyrells, and hundreds of others — and crowned herself queen over the ashes. Dany finally sailed for Westeros. She and Jon allied against a common enemy, became something more than allies, and Jon learned he was actually Aegon Targaryen, the true-born heir Dany didn't know she had. North of all of it, the Night King breached the Wall riding a dead dragon.

The Wall, standing since the Age of Heroes, fell in a single night.

V305 AC

The Long Night, and the Reckoning

At Winterfell, the army of the dead broke against the North's last defenders until Arya Stark put a dagger through the Night King and unmade every wight with him. There was one war left. King's Landing surrendered its bells the moment Dany's army reached the gates — and she burned it anyway, block by block, with a city full of people who had already given up. Jon, the one person she trusted, killed her rather than watch her go on. Drogon melted the throne she died for and carried her off. Nobody in that room wanted to sit in it after that.

Dany took the city she'd crossed the world for and lost everything else doing it.

The Ledger of Houses

8 great houses, 1 watch

Stark

Restored

Lost a father, a mother, a king in the north, and a home — got the North's independence, a queen in Sansa, and a king in Bran back for it.

Targaryen

Extinguished

The last dragon queen conquered a continent and died in the ashes of its capital, killed by the last man who loved her.

Lannister

Destroyed

Tywin, Joffrey, Cersei, and Jaime all died by family hands or family schemes. Tyrion is the only one left, and he's holding someone else's kingdom together now.

Baratheon

Extinct

Robert, Renly, Stannis, Joffrey, Tommen — three brothers and two sons, none left standing, the line ends with them.

Greyjoy

Reformed

Euron's grab for the throne died with him at the Sack of King's Landing. Yara took the Iron Islands back, quietly, and kept them.

Tyrell

Annihilated

Margaery and Loras died in Cersei's wildfire; Olenna took poison from Jaime rather than a siege. No one carries the name forward.

Martell

Broken

Oberyn died proving Tywin's man guilty and losing anyway. His widow's revenge plot cost Dorne its ruling family and its seat at the table.

Night's Watch

Held the line

Manned by criminals and outcasts for eight thousand years for exactly one night. When it mattered, it held — barely, and not for free.

Seven Who Mattered Most

principal players

Jon Snow

bastard → lord commander → king in the north → kinslayer → exile

Died once for keeping his word and again, in every way that mattered, for breaking it a second time — killing the woman he loved to stop the queen she'd become.

Daenerys Targaryen

last dragon → khaleesi → breaker of chains → queen of ashes

Freed every slave city she conquered and burned the one city that had already surrendered to her, the moment mercy stopped feeling like enough.

Cersei Lannister

queen consort → regent → sole ruler → buried under her own capital

Won every round she played dirty and lost the game entirely, dying in the cellar of a castle she'd have burned herself rather than surrender.

Tyrion Lannister

disgraced dwarf → hand of the king → kinslayer → hand again

Talked his way out of every trial but his own conscience, and ended the story doing the one job he was actually good at, for a king he didn't choose.

Arya Stark

a list → no one → someone again → the girl who killed death

Trained to erase herself, chose to stay Arya Stark instead, and used everything she'd learned to end the one enemy no army could beat.

Sansa Stark

hostage → pawn → survivor → queen in the north

Learned statecraft from the worst people in Westeros — Cersei, Littlefinger, Ramsay — and was the only one of their students who ended up wearing a crown.

Bran Stark

a boy who fell → the three-eyed raven → king of six kingdoms

Stopped being entirely human somewhere north of the Wall and was handed the throne precisely because he no longer wanted it.

The Realm, After

305 AC, and onward
  • The Six KingdomsUnited under King Bran the Broken, chosen by the surviving lords for having, as Tyrion put it, the best story — and no appetite left for the throne itself.
  • The NorthIndependent by the new king's first act, with Sansa Stark crowned Queen in the North in her own right.
  • The Small CouncilRebuilt around Tyrion as Hand, with Brienne, Davos, Bronn, and Samwell Tarly holding the offices that used to be won by murder.
  • The WallStill standing, still watched — though after the Night King, no one is entirely sure what for.
  • Jon SnowPardoned into exile, sent back to the Night's Watch and beyond it, north with the Free Folk, Tormund, and Ghost.
  • Arya StarkSailed west of Westeros, past every map anyone in the Seven Kingdoms had ever drawn.
